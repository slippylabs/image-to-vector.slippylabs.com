Tracing walks the cracks between pixels rather than the pixels themselves, so every contour lands on the integer grid and two colours that share a border share the exact same edge — that is why the colour output has no hairline seams between regions. Outlines are simplified with Ramer–Douglas–Peucker, then fitted with cubic Béziers by Schneider's algorithm; anything that turns sharper than the corner threshold is kept as a hard corner instead of being rounded off. EPS and DXF both put their origin at the bottom-left while images count rows downward, so both exports are mirrored on the way out — the kind of detail that produces a file which opens perfectly and is upside down. DXF is written as R2000 with one layer per colour; polylines import into anything, splines keep the curves exact but some budget laser software ignores them.
